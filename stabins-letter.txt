Sheriff Gee,

I saw the video and read the stories on the Tampa Bay Fox Channel 13 web site about the arrest by Deputy Kevin Stabins of a woman who was just trying to make sure her father was safe after a heart attack. I appreciate that you have done a lot to reduce the need for citizen complaints against your deputies, and that misconduct is rare, but it seems that Deputy Stabins has done this before. I commend your Internal Affairs people for suspending him. I ask that you give this letter to him, to hopefully clarify for him the seriousness of his offense.

Mr. Stabins,

Your job as an officer of the peace, is to uphold your oath of office, to protect and defend the Constitutions of the United States and of Florida. Those constitutions, and the governments that they form, exist for one and only one purpose, to preserve the liberty of the American people. As long as you're doing that job, helping us to maintain our liberty, I salute you.

On the night that you pulled over a woman who was attempting to go to the aid of her father, apparently leaving the hospital after recovering from a heart attack, the proper way to preserve her liberty would have been to realize that she needed an escort. In the America I want to live in, you would have cleared her way, and helped her to find her father.

In the America that appeared to be in your head on the night in question, you apparently considered enforcing the "law" to be more important than preserving the peace. There's a name for such officers. They're called jack-booted thugs.

There are also names for the crimes of man-handling someone and forcing her to go with you, without proper authority, without an actual crime having occurred. They're called assault and kidnapping. They are felony offenses, and rightly so. Kidnapping is a capital offense in many jurisdictions.

I hope that you can find in your heart true remorse for your actions. I hope that you will express that remorse, in writing, to the victim of your violent attack. If not, I demand that you leave public service, immediately and forever.

Bill St. Clair
