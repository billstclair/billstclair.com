Mmmmm, Pound Cake
Report on the 2003 Liberty Round Table conclave
by
Sunni Maravillosa

Believe it or not, that's what a conclave attendee said about the military's rendition of that cake -- as well as a couple of other dessert rations. Even more astonishing to those of us in on the conversation, the individual uttering this phrase placed hand on heart and looked skyward, adding emphasis to the exclamation. The happy utterance henceforth became a shorthand among some for expressing pleasure about something -- and it was frequently used with respect to this year's Liberty Round Table conclave itself.

At the beginning, it didn't seem that we were going to have any happy campers. As we were packing to head west, word came from our friend and co-conspirator Elias, of The Mental Militia, that our arrangements for a camping site had fallen through. Not knowing exactly where we were heading, we set off, hoping the problem would be resolved with a minimum of fuss and bother to the friends and Knights who'd be traveling from across the U.S. to the conclave.

Elias came through in fine style, lining up another site not too far from the first in about 18 hours. We schemed by cell phone on notifying everyone, and making final arrangements, and called those we could with new directions. Good thing we did, too, as we weren't the first to arrive at our new camp site.

And what a camp site it was! Up on a mountainside, nestled in an aspen grove, was a cleared spot that our hosts made available to us. They'd intended it to be a camping site for their family and friends, complete with electricity, spring water, and a covered kitchen, but completing the projects languished -- until they agreed to host our conclave. When we arrived, they were working on various details, and by that evening, running water was established. When they learned of our plan to set up a heated shower stall, another plan was formed, and soon a large scaffold appeared, upon which was set a 300-gallon plastic tank. That shower was wonderful -- conveniently located, discreetly rigged for privacy, but so that no one need miss any conversation while showering! The next day saw electricity come to the kitchen, so that the ancient refrigerator was able to provide its services once more.

Our host family did much more than provide land and amenities, however. Almost each morning, some wonderful creation appeared in the kitchen -- fresh-baked banana bread and raspberry jam, tapioca pudding that was the best I've ever had, fresh goat's milk -- all courtesy of the beautiful lady of the ranch. She also provided vegetables for dinners, and almost the entire celebration dinner -- but I'm getting ahead of myself. Our hosts also provided stimulating companionship and conversation throughout the conclave -- a better fit for the LRT could not be found.

Elias went on a porta-pottie raid to bring toilet facilities to our gathering, and recounted a hilarious tale of his mission. Later, when it became obvious that our group was more full of you-know-what than he'd expected, some of us conducted another raid that was less adventuresome in some ways, but nonetheless a fun afternoon lark filled with happy companionship for those who participated.

Our gun guru was in attendance again, and his classes filled with participants interested in improving their skills. For the most part, the "old hands" sat out so that those who hadn't been in a course with him could get the full benefit of doing so, and from the reports I heard, they learned a lot. Another high-caliber highlight was the availability of a .50 caliber rifle for those interested in things that really go "boom!". The Snolfs call it the "big-ass gun", and I heard no one take issue with that description. Judging from the comments and wide grins, however, no one was disappointed either. Perhaps it was the extra touch provided by the "have a nice day" inscribed on the muzzle brake.

The company was, by all accounts, the best feature of this year's conclave. It was a good balance of familiar friends and new faces -- including more college-age individuals than ever. Many were buoyed by the presence of so many concerned and intelligent younger individuals -- not to mention the XX features one was bestowed with. Our conclave was graced with the presence of several writers, including the best libertarian newspaperman around and the usually-reclusive Claire Wolfe (who, to our astonishment, announced her plans to join our happy gathering in advance). A FIJA founder and former libertarian presidential candidate were in attendance, as was at least one state LP rep. Since the risk of forest fires was too high, no flaming s'mores were produced, so the DAM debate went ammo-less again this year (if it even happened this year). A philosopher-wanderer and the head of an international pro-freedom organization also joined us. LRT regulars in attendance included our youthful gun genius, two lawyers (plus one more -- how did we stand them all?! ;-) , the North Pole (almost) contingent, the insightful llama rancher, and the usual "support crew" who never seem to volunteer for that duty but who always come through cheerfully and way above and beyond the call. Ward did up a bear chili (courtesy of the Alaskans, whose adventures getting the bear down to Montana I didn't hear) that was enjoyed by most campers (the snolfs were among the few who judged it "too spicy").

This being a meeting of free spirits, conversation seemed to be almost always waxing, save for the mornings I was up before dawn, casting my cyber-nets for freedom related news. However, the gloriously bright stars -- and several meteorites -- kept me company and provided needed perspective on the importance of my work. So did the supercharged coffee a dear friend provided me with each morning. The ability to work online in such a remote location -- via cell phone and car-battery power running a laptop -- was a testament to the ingenuity of man and machine. I'll be singing praises until we get the bill ;-) . Thanks to fellow traveler Tom Ender for his invaluable help in getting Freedom News Daily out in fine style, with only a few delays to show for my wanderings.

But back to the diverse free spirits ... in a group of cats the size we had -- by one estimate, almost 40 individuals wandered through our camp at some point -- planning of any sort is right out, and the possibility for friction virtually guaranteed. Plans that were made got tweaked -- sometimes to everyone's satisfaction, but sometimes not -- and most were very gracious about inevitable changes. Disagreements seemed to be taken mostly in a spirit of acceptance of legitimate differences among liberty lovers. One of the plans that got led astray was that for the celebration dinner. The opportunity to avail ourselves of the owner's sweat lodge proved irresistible to some conclavers, and those festivities pushed the dinner to a rather late hour. DLT delayed his speechifying until the next day's lunch, and my own remarks on creating a culture of freedom were cut short, but still seemed well received.

Rather than pontificate, Don Lobo unveiled a new order of LRT Knight: the Defenders. They areKnights of Non-Aggression whom we know have been "walking the talk" in working for freedom. Fewer Knight Defenders were present than we'd have wanted, but those present were called up for a ceremony and given a certificate that read generally as follows:

For conduct in keeping with the finest tradition of the Knights of Non-Aggression the Liberty Round Table presents this Order of the Defenders of Freedom award to ___ Who shall from this time on be recognized with the title of: "Knight of Non-Aggression, Defender of Freedom," or more simply as: Defender. May it bring him honor and distinction wherever His Life takes him.

Individuals named Defenders are: Elias Alias; Carl Bussjaeger; Susan Callaway; Jeffrey "The Hunter" Jordan; and Alberto Mingardi. Congratulations to each of them, and heartfelt thanks.

One of the more somber moments of the conclave came at its inaugural meeting. The Hunter brought out a bottle of fine single-malt scotch, and those of us gathered around the lantern-cum-fire offered a toast to missing friends. Our thoughts were most directed toward Dan Weiner, who's in very poor health, our dear Lady Susan whose health is also fragile, and Carl, the gentle bear whose good company was sorely missed. As always, the goodbyes began too early, and as the group thinned, I was left with a happy sense of accomplishment, many good memories, and a vast sadness that this oasis of reality that we've managed to create with a fair amount of success each summer for the past seven years is so ephemeral. I've heard from several folks -- newcomers and regulars alike -- that this year's conclave was a great success, possibly the best so far.

Mmmmmmm, pound cake ...
