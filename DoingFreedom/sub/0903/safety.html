"Safety" - Overkill and Deception
by
Susan Callaway, RN

I have bad news for some of you: life is full of risk, and nothing you or the government can do about it can remove all of that risk. This goes double for kids. You can wrap them up in a blanket and stuff them in a barrel or anything you want, but each and every choice, and every action you take has some element of risk. For every action there is a potential reaction you are not going to like. Live with it.

But - we all do want to take precautions to make things as safe as it is reasonably possible, of course. The key here is "reasonably". If you are the one taking those precautions, you can weigh the risk against the benefit of whatever is proposed and make a decision. Some people make bad choices, but most people quickly learn to analyze the situation and usually guess right. That is, of course, if they are free to do so.

The miracle is that, left to our own devices, we usually make the right choice and if we goof, we live to learn and do it better next time.

Unfortunately, far too many people have become convinced that a forced blanket of predetermined, mandated actions are better and "safer" than the use of our own God given intelligence and judgment. The part that makes no sense at all is how this idea persists in the face of the fact that no net gain in real "safety" can be demonstrated, no matter how many rules and "safety devices" are foisted off on us.

"Child proof 'safety' caps
"Child proof 'safety' caps are a perfect example. The common joke is that ONLY a child can get them off the containers. Too often this is true, but the real danger lies in two things. In the first place, most people can't get the caps off easily and so they tend to leave them very loose or off permanently. This not only circumvents any safety the ordinary cap would afford, but in the case of medications, also exposes them to air and dust, thus reducing the effectiveness or safety of the medication for the person who needs it. Second, some of those who DO use the 'safety' cap have a false sense of security and do not take the ordinary precautions to keep dangerous things out of the hands of children. Net gain for 'safety caps': zero.

All of the above goes into manic overdrive when it comes to medical supplies and devices.

Just for example, I've been a nurse for about 20 years. I've handled needles and sharp objects in every conceivable situation. I've been stuck twice, and both times it was because of my own stupidity. I learned not to do that anymore. Period. But now we pay literally millions of dollars every year for 'safety' needles which do nothing to prevent stupidity. The trend toward a needless system for most things has been real progress, but the cost is very high.

Individual and double packaging are very expensive too. Intended to cut down on the chance of contamination, they have their place, but overkill is common. I just opened a bottle of ordinary pain relief tablets. A paper box, a plastic film seal, a 'childproof' cap, another seal under that, and a wad of cotton I couldn't get out without a spoon handle. By the time I got to them, my headache had a headache. Hospital and professional medical supplies are even worse.

That would be fine, but all that costs big bucks, and nobody has any choice but to pay it.

Labels and tags.
Here the joke is that those who really need the warnings are the least likely to read them. Amusing, but also true. When was the last time you had to read the warning on a cup of take out hot coffee to know not to spill it on yourself - or hold it between your knees as you drove away? If you raised your hand, you may leave now because you are never going to "get it".

Do you really need a label to tell you not to allow your 2 year old to play near the fire? A 2 year old doesn't belong near an open flame and I don't care what she is wearing! Ditto for all the labels on children's toys and so forth. You might appreciate the findings of an independent testing lab or consumer group, but do you really need to be forced to pay for that information attached to every last balloon in a party pack? Why can't you choose what information you wish to pay for? Because the government and the tort suits have taken all choice and discernment away from us and invested it in the "experts".

Net gain? I'm not sure if any children are really safer now than they ever were. It's hard to prove anything else because all of the studies are done by the people trying to prove that the labels do save lives. They just have not explained how that happens when nobody but themselves actually reads them. But, by law, those labels must be on every single thing made, and we get to pay for it.

Think of that the next time you buy a small snack at the gas station. Look at the label, and realize that it is against the law to sell it if it doesn't have that label. Ever buy a big box of bagged snacks and see the label on each item that proclaims: "Not labeled for individual sale"? That's what it means. I suppose that the loophole that allows that shocking situation will be closed promptly now. Can't have your kids eating from those bags in school lunches too far away from the parent package with the proper 'label'... now can we.

Then there are the 'safety' devices that wind up being real killers in and of themselves! Car air bags come instantly to mind. They have killed more children than any other 'safety device' I can think of. Why are they still in all new cars then? Why can't people turn them off when small children are riding in the car? Why isn't there any real choice in the matter? Because the 'experts' know so much better than we do about what we want and need.... at least they think they do. Unfortunately, they have the weight of law to force their judgment on the rest of us.

There are many other examples, with motorcycle helmets being the most controversial, but the premise is the same. Either you use the brains God gave you, or you let the nanny state "take care of you". They want you to think they can. You can't have it both ways.
