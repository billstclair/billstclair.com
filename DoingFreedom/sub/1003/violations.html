Who, Me?
by
Carl Bussjaeger

I recently received an interesting query on one of our articles:

"hey -------- what is your other name?" (Identifying data removed to protect the privacy of the letter-writer.)

My first reaction to this inquiry was mixed: Amazement and anger that someone would be clueless or rude enough to ask that someone he thought was using a protective nym break cover.

Then I just got pissed.

This person felt that asking a person to break cover in an unencrypted communication was just fine, because the writer could decline, or ask for the inquirer's PGP key. Maybe the writer doesn't mind giving up his nym.

People use pseudonyms for reasons they deem to be good. Unless the person outright states that her use of a nym is not for privacy purposes, you should assume from the start that a presumed nym is de facto evidence of a desire for privacy: If she wants you to know her name, she would have told you instead of using a nym. Respect the writer's need for privacy.

If you honesty believe you have some compelling need to know the person's "real" identity -- a reason that overrides the her own need -- go about it a way that protects her from other people.

  • Encrypt your request so that someone monitoring communications won't pick up clues.
      I provided my own PGP public key on this site just for such sensitive situations.
  • Include your own PGP public key with your original request.
      The very use of nym must be taken as a desire for privacy (sound familiar?); no one should have to ask for the resource to maintain privacy.
  • Provide verifiable bona fides with your request, so the person can be sure that you are someone he wants to talk to.
      Anyone can claim to know <insert random activist's name>; give him something he can really check.
  • Be polite. You are asking a huge favor; you don't have a right to know anyone's "true name."

    Just because you may consider your own nym to be unimportant, maybe just a casual nickname, doesn't mean the same is the case for other people. In some branches of the freedom movement, some people feel the need to protect themselves through the use of nyms. Sometimes it's merely to avoid embarrassment at work. It might be to avoid law enforcement scrutiny (highly probable with DF! writers, given the sort of material we publish). They might be on the run. maybe they simply wanted a new life. But in every case, the very fact that they seem to be using a nym should be the intelligent person's clue that they don't want to share their "real" identities. You don't have to ask them; by using nyms, they already told you, "No!"

    Even I don't ask my nymmed writers who they are. I just ask for some channel to pay them. If one chooses to tell me his "real" name, that's his business; I don't need it, I don't ask. Check our writer's guidelines: I encourage writers to create nyms.

    Show a little respect for privacy, please.
