Crashing the State:
Activism for All Ages, in All Nations
by
Sunni Maravillosa

One of my favorite activist groups is Bureaucrash. Originally begun as a guerilla activism group to help spread pro-market and pro-freedom messages on college campuses, its highly effective memes are spreading beyond that demographic and reaching others. Their greatest success to date was the recent counterprotest in Cancun at the WTO talks.

While more staid free-marketeers were trying to engage the WTO wonks to try to persuade them of the folly of their statist, collectivist ways, the Bureaucrashers set out to tweak the protestors. And they did, in fine style, with a variety of very clever techniques that very often turned the protestors' tables against themselves. As one 'crasher reported to me, it was very eye-opening to see their reactions to the Bureaucrashers ... but let's not get ahead of the story.

Bureaucrash had organized the Cancun protest beginning months earlier, enlisting support from 'crashers around the U.S. and in Mexico (yes, there's a Bureaucrash cell in Mexico -- and Canada too!), creating slogans and developing ideas to execute. But once there, they discovered that reacting to the protestors' activities provided the best responses. A very effective technique proved to be their video interviews -- a crasher simply took a video camera around and taped the supposed anti-capitalists confessing how they enjoyed their Coca-Cola and Pizza Hut, or admitting that they're lazy and want to be supported by others.

Another video guerilla activist opportunity was used to great advantage when a Sierra Club activist being interviewed by U.S. television became irritated by crashers behind him waving signs that read, among other things, "Free Trade Now" and "Free Trade is Fair Trade". The Crasher-in-Chief, Jason Talley, wrote an amusing blog entry about the stunt.

Their best-known stunt was pulled off by 'crashers who figured out the hotel where leftist NGO-types advocating "fair trade" were staying. They hung flyers that looked like "do not disturb" doorknob hangers on their doors, pointing out that the hypocrites' chamber maids were being paid about 25 cents to clean their rooms, and asking them if they were making arrangements to pay their maids an extra, "fair" wage.

My favorite stunt was their Operation Crash-a-Cola. They strategically placed a soda stand where all the anti-globalist protestors had to pass by during their big march. At the stand they offered Coke, Sprite, and bottled water for two prices: a free trade price of $0.50 or the fair trade price of $2. Two-thirds of their supposedly fair-trade customers chose the free trade price -- while engaging in capitalism with a pro-capitalist group selling an eeevil multinational company's products! Of course, when enough of them figured out what was going on, some formed a human chain around the stand, preventing free trade from happening -- just like so many state governments around the world.

Reports from the Cancun protest make it obvious the Bureaucrashers had a lot of fun with their protests, while also helping to get out important ideas about what real free trade is. Several 'crashers said that at the beginning of the protest, they were assumed to be "globalophobics" too, but by the end of the WTO talks, the protestors had begun to recognize them and wouldn't take their flyers. It appeared that they were quite demoralized by the idea of having some of their favorite protest techniques turned against them -- and by the idea of having any objections raised to their collectivist views at all. All this creative 'crashing earned the Cancun protestors a lot of media attention, and helped Bureaucrash get its pro-freedom message out to a large, worldwide audience.

Not content to rest on its laurels, Bureaucrash is keeping the heat on high. While I can't list all their activities here, a visit to the web site will provide anyone interested in their style of activism with enough ideas and materials to last a long while. Daily dispatches in the left column keep one abreast of recent news events and other interesting stuff, while the right column offers cell information, reports on past events, and lots more. The site is chock-full of posters, flyers, and other propaganda material that, when read carefully, will make any statist's brain's gears grind. One comment I found particularly interesting was left by a 65-year-old man who groks, and supports, what Bureaucrash is doing.

Their next big project is Operation Miami Mayhem, November 17-21 in sunny Florida. (Who says these 'crashers don't know how to choose good protest sites?) They'll be taking it to the anti-free-traders again, at the Free Trade Area of the Americas meeting. Given their success in Cancun, you can be sure it'll be an interesting five days. Planning for the protest is just getting underway, so there's plenty of time for you to get involved, too. If you can't, well, don't wait for them to come to you -- visit their site, check out their great ideas, and do some crashing yourself!
