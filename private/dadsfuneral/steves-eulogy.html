[Given by Steve St. Clair at the funeral of Col. William W. St. Clair, 6 December, 2001]

It's great to see you all here gathered to celebrate Dad's life. I hope you'll bear with me because I'd like to take this opportunity to say a few words TO Dad. But first, I'd like to share with you some of the memories I recalled while trying to choose those words. A poem or lymerick would have been Dad's choice - I'll always remember his concentration followed by a wry smile as he'd recite his carefully scripted verses.

But for me it has to be just a few words because that's all Dad and I used with each other. I think because we're both hopeless romantics we wanted to limit our conversation to just pertinent information so as not to threaten our machismo.

For example - a conversation on the 8 hr drive to Jackson to go elk hunting would go something like this:

SILENCE About ½ way to Laramie:

Dad, "Goats, 2 oclock." Me, "Yup, 2 of um."

Dad, "Bucks?" Me, "Yup, one real big."

SILENCE We'd approach the West Laramie Fly Store. Dad,"Ice cream?" Me,"Great!"

SILENCE Toward lunch (maybe near Rock Springs) after a few stray comments about wild carcasses or the elk migration:

DAD, "Djou eat yet?" Me, "no, djou?"

Dad, "no, lesqueet."

But the silences were never uncomfortable. We were both content, knew how the other felt and needed words only to pass on relevant facts.

I remember when I was 15, we went bighorn sheep hunting in the Absorakas. We'd hiked in quite a ways and hunted for a day or so when Dad sprained one of his old basketball weakened ankles and could hardly walk. We had pitched our tent near a stream where he rested while I played camper and caretaker. I'd poke my head in the tent and make a long speech like, "trout won't take my flies." To which he might reply,"Stoop to worms?" and I'd manage "think about it." He never complained or seemed worried so I actually had a good time

One morning we awoke at sunrise. Just outside our tent flap, maybe 10 feet away, was a buck deer still in partial velvet, nibbling the grass by last night's campfire. It was one of the most beautiful things I'd ever seen and the look of wonder and appreciation in Dad's eye was all I needed - we never really had to talk about it.

So I knew my words to Dad today should be short - maybe 3 or 4. My first thought was "Thanks Dad, for life." Not for giving me life - although Dad helped, Mom did most of that hard work. Not for bringing me up so well - although Dad helped...Mom did most of the nurturing, teaching and even punishing (I think Dad was too much of a softee to do that hard job.)

No...it would be "Thanks Dad," for instilling in me the love of life. He had an enduring love for all things living, for the natural world, for the challenges of life and for the sheer fun of being alive. Each time he chose a new challenge he would jump in whole hog.

He decided to box - he won the golden gloves;

To fly fish - he spent a summer in Newfoundland with one of the all time masters;

To pistol shoot - he set national records;

To fly sail planes - he amassed 3 diamond awards.

Sometimes, he'd seem almost maniacal in his quest but then that little smile of his would appear and you could see his sheer joy for the quest itself.

I remember brother Bill's and my first fly fishing attempts in the Firehole river. You could almost taste Dad's excitement about those trout calling to him - but I strongly remember his patience showing me that fly knot one MORE time or untangling my umpteenth wayward backcast.

Dad's enthusiasm was often infectious. Once, he convinced my wife to go for a ride in his sail plane. They had to hold some tight curves to stay in a thermal and Elizabeth found herself looking for the air sickness bag. When they returned to earth, Dad having given her his favorite hat in place of the absent airsickness bag, Elizabeth announced,"That was the most fun I've ever had while barfing!"

Of course, to truly enjoy life you have to have all the right equipment. Dad had a firm grip on this concept and I certainly was a good learner. Unfortunately, all the best equipment doesn't always mean you'll be the best. Dad had a whole library of how to golf books and even though you only hit one golf ball at a time he had 5 or 6 drivers. Nonetheless, like most of us, he never quite mastered golf. But the best round I ever saw him play was when I visited last summer. His eyes were bad enough he saw 2 golf balls and had to guess where the real one was. His balance was bad enough he could hardly get in and out of the golf cart. But he'd address the ball and usually hit it straight and square. Returning to the cart, I could tell he still wished for that 200 yard shot, but he'd give me his wry smile and say "getting closer."

PAUSE

Like with that deer in the Absorakas I'm certain Dad doesn't need to hear "thanks dad, for life." He knows I have that gift he gave me that whenever I'm standing by a mountain stream I can see his reflection just laying out that perfect cast; that when I rest near a grove of quakies I can see his smile as he pulls out a tin of some mystery meat and says "Djou eat yet?" And that each time I see a hawk holding a thermal I can see Dad soaring with that hawk, maybe with his Dad, Rex St. Clair, in the second seat. And Dad has on his favorite hat which in a pinch, can double as a barf bag.

No, the words thanks are not necessary because Dad will always be with me and he can see that look of wonder and appreciation in my eyes.

So...what 4 words do I have for Dad today. Being a romantic like him they have to be those words that he and I rarely uttered as we exchanged facts. We both knew they were there under the surface but today I want to say them aloud - I love you Dad. And that love will keep you in me always.
