[Given by John St. Clair at the funeral of Col. William W. St. Clair, 6 December, 2001]

By many peoples standards my father was not a Great man. He didn't find a cure for cancer invent a source of endless free power or win a Nobel prize. When dad did decide to do something he would put his heart and soul into it. There must be twenty books on how to be a better tennis player, manuals on the use of every golf club in his bag and every gizmo an gadget known to man. In these fields dad was a Good man.

When it came to the outdoors and the love of hunting and fishing, things came a little more natural to dad. Granted he still had every book , the finest equipment and an impeccable knowledge the land and nature. You can see by the trophy fish elk moose and antelope on the walls of the basement at the house that in this area dad was a Very good man.

There was also a very loving side of dad. The story of his proposal to mon, his patience with myself and my sibling as he taught us something new and his heart warming smile when we did something well showed that. I can never recall a time when dad actually yelled at me in anger. In all of this dad was a Gentle man.

Dad's true love, besides for my mother, whom I know he loved dearly, I think would have to have been flying, I was lucky enough to spend a lot of time with him when he was flying sail planes and the short stint when he owned a single engine Cherokee. I remember soaring with him once and he was doing all kinds of aerobatics,, loops, lazy eights shins and I din''t think I have ever seen him happier. We flew the Cherokee down to boulder one day and he had to land in a thunderstorm with some pretty strong cross winds. I was scared to death. He took one shot at the landing and had to pull off and try again. I sweat we were flying sideways down the runway when he finally touched down and taxied to the hanger. He tuned to me rather non challantly and said " that was kind of sticky, huh?" He loved it and I have never been more impressed. When it came to flying dad was an incredible man.

With all that said, I truly think the measure of a man is not how many trophy heads he has or how many medals or awards he has received or how many by the seat of your pants landings he has made, but instead by how good he is in his heart and how many hearts he has touched on his journey through life. I think all of you would agree with me that by this measure my dad was a great man
