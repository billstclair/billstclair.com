Vietnam hero added to Aviation Wall of Fame

James Lehtonen, shown above during the Vietnam War, was awarded a Distinguished Flying Cross. Lehtonen flew 619 combat sorties during his two tours in Vietnam. (Photo courtesy of Barbara Lehtonen)

By STEPHEN STACY, Journal Ishpeming Bureau

NEGAUNEE — Remembered as a shy, studious young man by those who knew him while growing up in Negaunee, James Lehtonen didn’t seem the model of a typical, brash U.S. Air Force fighter pilot.

Certainly, he didn’t strike people as someone who would earn a Distinguished Flying Cross for extraordinary achievement. But while flying combat sorties over the skies of Vietnam in 1967, that’s exactly what he did.

Now his name will be remembered at a Sawyer International Airport memorial wall recognizing the contributions of local aviators.

According to his younger sister Barbara Lehtonen, her brother always had an interest in flying and spent many hours building model airplanes at their home on Tobin Street in Negaunee.

That early interest in flying led Lehtonen to become a member of the Michigan Tech University Air Force ROTC program. Shortly after graduating from MTU in 1955, he was commissioned as a second lieutenant.

Lehtonen joined the Air Force in 1956 and served for 20 years, retiring as a Major in 1976. He accumulated 5,000 hours of flight time and two tours of duty in Vietnam. During his first tour in 1963, he flew 510 combat hours as a Forward Air Controller, flying the Cessna TO1D/L-19 “Bird Dog” on reconnaissance missions. During his second tour, he flew the F100 Super Sabre jet for a total of 350 combat hours. In all, he logged a total of 860 combat hours and 619 combat sorties.

A mission on March 24, 1967, led to Lehtonen being awarded the Distinguished Flying Cross. The award citation reads in part, “Captain Lehtonen led a night close air support mission in defense of friendly ground forces who were in heavy contact with a hostile force of unknown strength and despite the highly adverse conditions of darkness and continuous hostile ground fire, Captain Lehtonen’s flight suppressed the heavy hostile fire on the friendly positions, allowing successful evacuation of the friendly forces with a minimum of casualties.”

Lehtonen earned a total of 24 Air Medals in two decades of service to his country — several were awarded for meritorious service while flying combat missions under extremely hazardous conditions in Vietnam.

Shortly after retiring, Lehtonen took a position as a charter pilot in Myrtle Beach, S.C.

On April 7, 1980, Lehtonen was scheduled to fly his Cessna 401 on a routine flight from his home base in Myrtle Beach to meet his boss in Cincinnati, Ohio.

While flying through dense clouds, rain and considerable wind over Grandfather’s Mountain near Linville, N.C., Lehtonen’s plane disappeared and was presumed lost due to the inclement weather conditions. Four days later, the plane wreckage and Lehtonen’s body were located about 50 feet from the mountain’s peak. He left behind his wife Marie and daughters Karla, Tracy and Susan.

More than 20 years later, while waiting for a flight at the Sawyer International Airport in Gwinn, Marion Olson — a friend of Lehtonen’s and a fellow Negaunee class of 1951 graduate — happened upon the Aviation Wall of Fame, which lists the names of local military and civilian aviators who have made significant contributions in the field of flight.

“I looked at the wall and noticed Jim’s name wasn’t up there,” Olson said. “I wondered why his name wasn’t on the wall.”

For months, Olson and several other members of the class of 1951 made a class project out of trying to get Lehtonen’s name added to the wall. Eventually, they realized that a family member must be involved for the Department of Defense to release military records, which were needed to verify Lehtonen’s aeronautical contributions. That’s when Olson called Lehtonen’s sister Barbara.

“I gave her the information and said ‘let’s get Jim’s name up on that wall,’” Olson said.

With help and emotional support from several of Lehtonen’s former classmates, Barbara began the lengthy process of securing and filing the documents critical to assuring Lehtonen’s rightful place on the wall.
