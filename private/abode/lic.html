Mission Statement

The Abode of the Message is a Sufi Order spiritual, residential community and a Sufi Order Center, the purpose of which is the transmission of the Sufi Message as presented by Pir-O-Murshid Inayat Khan. This purpose is fulfilled by members of the Center and of the community activity pursuing these teachings.

OUR SPIRITUAL LIFE

Sufism is a mystical path which embraces the essence of all religion, without dogma. The ten Sufi thoughts of Hazrat Inayat Khan give some idea of the Sufi perspective on life. Life itself is the Teacher. The rich inheritance we have received in the form of teachings from Hazrat Inayat Khan and Pir Vilayat are intended to shine a light on our daily experiences.

For those wishing to study the "Sufi Message" brought by Hazrat Inayat Khan or to dip into the Ancient Sufis or listen to meditations by Pir Vilayat, we have set up a corner in the Library as a "study center" with a cassette cabinet and tape recorders (please use the headphones provided), copies of the message volumes and helpful books explaining the Sufi path. The books in this section are not to be removed from the library.

The Sufi Order International is an esoteric school in which spiritual guidance is given on an individual basis. It recognizes that every person is unique and each one's unfoldment is different. Initiates in SOI may take on a spiritual guide who must have a developed intuition to be able to perceive the spiritual needs of another person and prescribe practices accordingly. Usually one takes a spiritual guide upon being initiated into the Sufi Order, but at the Abode we encourage everybody to meet regularly with a guide to explore those aspects of the teachings and basic practices which might be of most interest to them. Some people arriving at the Abode already have a spiritual guide elsewhere. There is no need to change in that case. People are authorized as spiritual guides by SOI. The Abode has an active retreat concentration which is overseen by the Retreat Coordinator. Individual retreats of any length may be arranged with that person who may becontacted through the programs office.

Community Spiritual activities are many and varied:

A. Prayers: Group prayers are held three times a day in the meditation hall. Morning 6:30 - 7:00, Noon 12:15 - 12:30, Evening 5:30 - 6:00. At different times in the Abode's history we have used different formats for prayers. We have taken turns in leading guided meditations, reading from the message volumes, chanting, silence, etc. At the end of our half hour meditation we join together in the prayers which Hazrat Inayat Khan gave.

B. Sunday - Universal Worship Service: The Universal Worship service is held every Sunday at 10:15 a.m. in the Meditation Hall. In this service we honor each of the major world religions, by lighting candles and reading from their scriptures. The service, designed by Hazrat Inayat Khan, is a good introduction to the Sufi tenet of the "Unity of Religious Ideals". For those interested in finding out more about the Universal Worship Service, please talk with a Cherag/Cheraga (people ordained to lead the worship service).

C. An Introduction to Meditation: This class precedes the Universal Worship at 9:30 a.m. on Sundays. Everyone is welcome.

D. SOI Class: is held every Thursday evening at 7:00 PM in the Meditation Hall. This class explores the teachings of Hazrat Inayat Khan as well as the teachings of Pir Vilayat.

E. Zikr Circle: A Zikr circle is held every Thursday at 8:00 p.m as a part of the SOI class.

F. The Healing Order: The Healing Circle is a ritual in the form of group prayer whereby healing is sent to those who have asked for their names to be included on our list. The services, held on Sundays after Universal Worship and on Wednesday evenings, are open to members and candidates. There is a half hour class on healing, which is open to everybody, on Wednesday evenings at 7:00. A list of healing conductors is on the bulletin board in Rezak.

G. The Ziraat Activity: Ziraat Lodges are generally held on the full and new moons and on the solstices and equinoxes. The Ziraat teachings were given by Hazrat Inayat Khan. Ziraat is a short ritual and also a life-long initiatory path. It uses the symbology of farming to describe the unfoldment of the soul. "The work" of the Ziraat Farmer is always work on one's own self, the farm. The destination of this path is the same as any other Sufi concentration, and yet it offers its own unique perfume.

H. Programs: The Programs office organizes and presents our public seminars. Our seminars and group retreats are presented on our mountain campsite in the summer and the Meditation Hall at other times. Check with the Programs Office -- located on the first floor of Vakil, for further details.

I. Sweat Lodges: are held from time to time. If you are interested in this or other native American religious activities, speak with the Ziraat coordinator.

J. All Other Classes: Anybody wishing to give a class of any nature in any of the Abode public spaces submits their request to the SOI Center Steering Committee which meets monthly, and is chaired by the appointed Center Representative.

Current lists of Representatives, Retreat Guides, Cheragas/ Cherags, Healing Conductors, Healing Order Members and persons holding leaderships for concentrations are posted on the bulletin board on the first floor of Rezak.

LIVING IN COMMUNITY AT THE ABODE

Living in community anywhere requires adjustments and commitment, living at the Abode requires a special commitment to a spiritual path -- a path that embraces the Sufi Message brought by Hazrat Inayat Khan. This path must, of course, be your own; however, there are common attitudes which make it possible for a person to live harmoniously at the Abode. One must feel an attunement to the spiritual teachings and ideals of Hazrat Inayat Khan and Pir Vilayat Khan.

The nature of human relationship is subtle and complex and in community this especially needs to be given the utmost attention and care. The following is a summary of our agreements which have helped us establish a clear understanding in our life together.

1. COMMITMENT AND EXPECTATION

A. Contributions to Community Life: The following commitments are expected of everyone residing in the community for more than a week:

1. Karma Yoga. Three hours work per week in the kitchen.

2. Work Morning. The first Saturday of the month is work morning. We gather at 9:00 a.m. for a short attunement and proceed with our various projects until lunch at 12:30. If you are unable to attend, a $24.00 fee will be added to your monthly bill if you do not do work day or alternate work within three weeks of the worked day for that month. If you do alternate work please notify the Abode administrator. (fee instituted as of 9/16/96)

B. Unacceptable Behavior. We all agree that the following is not conducive to harmonious community life and will result in expulsion from the community:

1. Illegal Drugs: are NOT allowed and will result in immediate expulsion from the Abode.

2. Physical Violence: resulting in damage to other persons or property is not allowed and may result in expulsion from the community.

3. Inappropriate Sexual Behavior: All sexual relations between adults should be with mutual consent and respect. Sexual intent toward a child will lead to immediate expulsion. Under all circumstances adults must make clear and appropriate boundaries with children.

4. Inappropriate Social Behavior: Consideration for others is felt to be the basis of any spiritual growth. If you cannot live harmoniously you may be asked to leave.

5. Possession of Fire Arms. No guns of any kind are allowed to be kept in Abode-owned spaces.

2. PUBLIC SPACES

Everything except your bedroom, house or apartment are considered public spaces.

A. Communications: In order to keep in tune with what is happening at the Abode, there are several communication channels.

1. The Bulletin and Chalk Boards: located outside the dining room contain important information, schedules and articles of interest to guests and community members. The chalk board is used for current information.

2. The Open Heart Newsletter: is a weekly exchange of information, lost and found, poetry, invitations, etc. All signed entries are welcome.

3. Let's Talk: Let's Talk Meetings occur as needed. We make use of a Talking Stick format, offering complete attentive listening to each speaker in turn without interruption or comment. These meetings are intended to support healthy communication and safe airing of issues and concerns of all natures.

3. Telephone and Mail: Please speak with the Office about rules and regulations regarding the phones and mail system. Only local calls may be made on the Dining Room phone. The Fatah phone is a typical pay-phone.

B. Dining Room Policy: Please: a) bring all plates, cups and silverware to the scullery; b) attempt to keep the noise level down; c) clean up after your children; and d) hang coats in the back hallway or on the hangers in the scullery. The back room is for quiet dining.

C. Cleaning: In general, the residents of each building are responsible for keeping the common areas of their building clean. Sign up on the building work charts to do assigned chores; these include: cleaning the hallways, bathrooms, and taking out the trash, among other things. House meetings may be held to implement these obligations. The residue from cooking individual meals or from any projects should be cleaned up as soon as possible. Parents are responsible for cleaning up after their children in the buildings and on the grounds.

D. Personal Belongings: are not to be left in any of the public areas. All personal belongings are to be stored and kept in personal rooms or storage areas.

E. Parties: in the main building complex are to be announced (dates and times) in the newsletter a week in advance and should have the approval of the people who live in the building where the party is to be held. Children's parties must have an adult sponsor. A time limit must be set - usually 11:00 p.m.

Each party must be put on the monthly calendar in the office to ensure that no conflicts in scheduling ensue.

F. Alcohol: is allowed in moderation, but use is discouraged in common areas. Be conscious of others who may be coping with alcohol addiction. Drunkenness is not permitted.

G. Sauna: The sauna is turned on Wednesday and Saturday nights for general use and on Friday nights for ladies. At all other times there is a $10 fee and arrangements should be made with the office in advance.

3. COMMUNITY HEALTH AND HYGIENE. Anyone living in community should be especially aware of any communicable disease since it could have a drastic effect on the whole community. Your responsible co-operation is necessary to safeguard the whole community.

A. Persons who have a cold, cough, upset stomach, etc., please try to keep away from the kitchen and avoid handling food.

B. Children with a communicable illness (e.g., chicken pox, measles, etc.) should be isolated until the incubation period is over. See the resident nurses for pertinent information.

C. Anyone with a skin infection should be careful about handling food, showering and using the sauna. Separate your towel from others. It is recommended that EVERYONE clean the bath or shower after use and sit on their towel in the sauna. If you have the slightest suspicion of athletes foot, wear flip-flops in the shower and sauna.

4. NOISE

A. Quiet Hours: Quiet Hours are from 10:00 p.m. to 8:00 a.m. Please remember the buildings are not sound-proofed very well, so even loud talking or slamming doors may be disturbing.

B. Audio Output: Audio equipment and television should be kept at a moderate level. Your door should be closed out of consideration of your neighbors. Music in the kitchen should be kept at a moderate level.

C. Retreat Cabins: The little cabins behind the Meditation Hall and at the campsite are retreat huts. Please keep silence around them. During group retreats, all residents are expected to respect the need of retreatants for silence and general quiet.

5. KITCHEN POLICIES: The Abode kitchen supplies meals and leftovers are available for general use. Certain items may be taken to rooms and houses in limited quantities. The current list of food items and amounts are posted on the Kitchen Bulletin Board. Items are limited due to the role the family has defined for the Kitchen as a supplier of basic needs at minimum cost to everyone.

A. Karma Yoga: All residents contribute 3 hours per week to either one meal preparation or two meal clean-ups each week. Everyone is responsible to get a replacement when they are away. One may either trade another person on the Karma Yoga Chart, pay a replacement or request the Kitchen to find a paid replacement. It is not necessary to get a replacement for absences from the Community of three weeks or longer, but the Kitchen Manager should be notified in advance. Requests for exemptions from Karma Yoga go to the Kitchen Manager or the Residence Committee.

B. Hygiene in the Kitchen: For community health and well-being, the following hygiene should be followed by everyone:

1. Cooks: a) Wash hands before preparing foods, b) tie long hair back, and c) taste foods from pots with separate spoons.

2. Cleanup crews: a) Fill the dishwasher with water and turn on the heater at least 15 minutes before using, b) use plenty of soap when washing pots and pans and plenty of hot water when rinsing them, c) scrub food stuff from corners and crevices of all pans, appliances and containers, and d) cover all leftover foods before placing in cooler.

3. Snack preparation: a) always use a serving spoon or scoop to take portions from containers; b) do not taste with fingers or take with hands, c) recover and replace foods, d) clean all counters, toasters, cheese slicer, blenders and pans, and e) children under 10 years old should always be accompanied by an adult when getting snacks.

4. People are asked to be conscious of the need to use utensils in the serving line rather than hands.

C. Cooks Priority: Please remember that during cooking periods in all probability the cooks will want to have the kitchen clear of people and personal food preparation. If you need something, please ask the cooks' permission.

PLEASE HELP CREATE A HARMONIOUS ATMOSPHERE IN THE KITCHEN, ESPECIALLY DURING MEAL PREPARATION.

D. Red Meat: No red meat is cooked in the main kitchen.

6. FIRE SAFETY

A. For safety: Be sure there is a working smoke detector on your floor or in your room; be sure your room or apartment has a fire ladder and you know how to use it to get out. Be sure not to park in Dowla or Rezak driveways overnight since they are our Fire Lanes. (See Parking and Vehicle Regulations, Section 10.C.)

B. Open Flames: With the exception of the Dining Room during mealtimes, the Meditation Hall during prayers and the fireplace in the Family Room while attended, no open flames (candles, etc.) are allowed. Please be conscious of fire hazards. For information concerning use of fire extinguishers and ladders, see the Fire Marshall.

C. Smoking: (As of 6/9/97) The Abode of the Message, its buildings and property are smoke free. Smoking is permitted in these two parking lots: the parking lot behind Vakil and the lower parking lot on the mountain. Please do not litter.

D. Kerosene Heaters: No kerosene heaters are allowed in the main buildings.

E. Heat: Everybody participated in stoking the furnace in buildings with wood furnaces. Please ask an experienced person about how to stoke. When stoking a stove, never leave any doors to heaters open, do not leave any combustible articles closer than three feet from the stove and put hot ashes outside in a non-combustible metal container. F. Electrical Appliances: No electrical appliances are allowed unless the electrical outlet to be used is checked by Abode Maintenance Coordinator. When approved, they must NEVER be used with extension cords. No electrical appliances should be left unattended.

7. HOUSING POLICY

Housing in rooms in the main building complex are coordinated by the Residence Committee. The Residence Committee posts a list of vacant or soon-to-be vacant rooms and individuals sign for the room(s) they would like to occupy. After being posted for a week, the Residence Committee will make a decision. Rooms are assigned primarily on the basis of seniority with special consideration for individual circumstances, such as health needs, full-time Abode employment, children, couples. A degree of flexibility is requested regarding room accommodations. Long term visitors and work exchange persons may be asked to move their room if a situation of greater need arises (determined by the Residence Committee). They will be given two weeks notice prior to the change. Decisions on which rooms are needed for rental purposes will be made by the Residence Committee. Residents are encouraged to share any room considerations with them.

The following guidelines are used to determine room status and rent:

A. Room Status: may change from time to time according to the Abode's needs. A ROOM'S STATUS IS DETERMINED AT THE TIME OF POSTING SO EVERYONE GETS A SHOT AT IT.

B. Second Room/Couples: An extra single room costs an additional $150 a month.

C. Children: Are included in the rent structure with his or her parent.

D. Abode Housing Allocation: Housing policy for apartments, houses and Abode-owned apartments is determined by Residence Committee and Executive. Circumstances which may be taken into consideration include: Abode financial needs; special needs including children; if the person is a member of the Sufi Order and is committed to the future of the Abode; if the person is a full-time Abode, Ægis or Sufi Order employee, etc. The Residence Committee will try to take all factors into consideration with the overall Abode vision.

When an apartment or house becomes available or for sale, it will be announced in the newsletter and posted on the bulletin board. In the event of strong disagreement with a decision, a person may apply to the Executive Committee for review of the decision.

E. Storage Space: There is storage space in Alim and elsewhere, but it is limited and for Residents only. When a person leaves the Abode, they must take their belongings. If they do not, a warning will be given, then all items will be removed and offered to residents or free store or Goodwill. All belongings in storage should be well labeled. Storage in the barn must be approved by the Executive Committee. The Abode does not provide insurance for personal belongings for anyone.

F. Your Room: (Please see Fire Safety, pg. 9)

1. Everyone at the Abode is expected to provide their own bedding and furniture. Put a note in the newsletter and check with the Abode administrator regarding any items remaining in a new room. Do not remove any articles or furniture from other rooms for personal use, especially blankets.

2. No person may store or place personal belongings outside their room or in any public space. A warning will be issued and then the items will be taken to the Goodwill.

G. Abode Owned Dwellings:

Appliances: Those who live in Abode-owned buildings or apartments are responsible for their own appliances (stove, refrigerator, etc.). The Abode will not supply or maintain appliances in individual apartments or buildings.

H. Individual Supplied Housing:

1. Lease: Houses at the Abode are generally purchased as a building to be owned by the individual with the land under the house to be rented under a 99 year lease agreement with the Abode. The lease agreement spells out many details and makes clear the rights of all parties.

2. Resale: In addition to the Leasehold Agreement: should a Leaseholder decide to sell the Leasehold interest, they may request, and the Executive Committee may decide to purchase, the Leaseholder's equity at the buyout price plus uncompounded interest based on the Consumer Price Index.

8. FINANCES

A. Rent Structure: Rent is due on the 1st of each month for the upcoming month. A late of $15.00 or 2% of the unpaid balance will be imposed on the 6th of the month. A letter will be sent out on the 6th to the person in arrears stating that they must meet with a member of Planning Finance and Maintenance Committee within 7 days of receiving that letter. If the person in arrears does not meet with the member of PF&M within the stated amount of time, that person will be asked to leave the community. One can get back into good standing by either paying the rent owed or creating a re-payment plan with the finance team. (passed 4/14/97) B. Amount: Rent amounts vary according to Planning, Finance and Maintenance Committee determinations and may also float according to budget agreements. Rent is composed of 4 areas: the space occupied, food, general operations and Family Member status. The exact amounts are determined by the annual budget The current rent structure was adopted on April 6, 2001. Please see rent structure attachment.

1. Family Member Resident: lives in Abode-owned spaces, has paid the $500 admission fee and has donated 70 hours labor, has been accepted as a resident by the Residence Committee.

2. Non Family Member Resident (candidate): lives in Abode-owned spaces, has expressed a desire to become a Family Member and has been accepted as such by the Residence Committee, but has not yet completed the steps listed above.

3. Seeker and Long Term Visitor: lives in Abode-owned spaces, has not declared an intention to work toward Family Member status.

4. First in leasehold: a person, as described above, who is providing his or her own housing at the Abode.

5. Second in Leasehold: a person living as a second individual in non-Abode owned spaces and pays for space as defined by current fee structure.

6. Beloved: lives with a resident and is present due to a relationship with a Resident in a non-Abode owned space provided by a resident but is not interested in becoming part of the Abode community. Beloveds are not responsible for Karma Yoga, work day or meetings.

7. Work Exchange: lives at the Abode on a trial basis exchanging 25 hours per week work for living at the Abode. Current Work Exchange Fees = $150/month

C. Sub-Letting: A Homeowner may elect to allow a room in his/her house to be used by an Abodian who would not normally be in that house. The room-user would still pay normal rent to the Abode. The Homeowner would get a rent reduction of up to $100 per month. A Homeowner may sublet their house through a process of negotiations with the Executive Committee and with the approval of the Residence Committee. In General the Abode would like to maintain the income the house was producing.

D. Away Fee Schedule: A person may go away with no payment for any length of time if they move their belongings from the room and give up their room rights.

The Residence Committee should be notified well in advance to retain a room during an absence. Applications should be made to the Executive Committee for a possible away fee reduction of up to $100. The Abode has the right to house others in rooms while people are away.

E. Room Renovation: Any costs incurred by an individual for the renovation of an Abode-owned room, apartment or house must get prior approval for reimbursement from the Executive Committee.

F. Visitors/Guests:

1. Anyone wishing to visit the Abode is encouraged to contact the office in advance to make arrangements. Anyone wishing to visit for more than one week must: contact the Programs Office, fill out a Admission questionnaire and meet with the Residence Committee (Mondays 5:00 - 6:00 p.m.) by signing up on the sheet by the chalkboard outside the dining room.

2. Definitions:

a. Short term visitor: anyone staying for less than one month and paying the current rate posted in the Office. Children under the age of three pay no fee and between 3 and 18 years pay 1/2 visitor fee.

b. Long Term Visitor: anyone staying for more than one month.

c. Beloved: someone who an intimate relationship with a Resident and who is not interested in Resident status at the Abode.

d. Personal Guest: a relative or close friend whom a Resident has asked to visit them.

3. Residents' Guests: may stay for the first three days for free. A guest who wishes to stay for more than one week should speak with the Residence Committee and arrange to participate in Karma Yoga. If a guest needs a room, then their staus will change and they should speak with the Abode administrator in the office about financial arrangements.

Guests who stay longer than three days in a person's room or house pay only for meals $10 per day.

Abode Residents are requested not to invite personal guests during seminars or group retreats held at the Abode (not at the campsite) because of limited housing.

If a Beloved stays longer than one cumulative month, they should meet with the Residence Committee to re-evaluate their relationship with the community. The Residence Committee will make an arrangement for some contribution to the Community, possibly in the form of participation in workdays, a special project and/or financial contribution (see Finances-Amount, pg. 12).

On subsequent visits the Beloved should maintain the agreement established (subject to periodic review). Residents are asked to keep the Residence Committee informed of their Beloved's presence and absence.

4. Long Term Visitors: Guests of long term visitors pay $10/night for the first three nights and after that the Current Fee. Immediate relatives may stay for free the first two nights.

5. Former Family Members: Former Family Members may stay for seven nights free each year and then pay the current fee after that or arrange work exchange through Residence. If a former member wishes to return to live at the Abode, they must reapply through the Residence Committee. Acceptance is not automatic. There will be a two month trial period after initial acceptance and feedback will be solicited from the other current Abodians before Family Member status can be reinstated. H. 9. CANDIDACY AND RESIDENT STATUS

After living at the Abode for at least six months one may apply to the Residence Committee to become a Candidate. Former Family Members are not automatically accepted. The criteria which determine appropriateness of Candidate status or renewal status are:

A. Candidate Status: Feedback will be solicited from the community and acceptance will be granted or denied based on this feedback. During the course of candidacy a Seeker is expected to give $500 membership fee and 70 hours of donated labor to the Abode in a concentration pre-approved by the Administrator of the Abode. From the date of applications onward, the difference between Family Member Fees and Non Family Member fees of each month's rent will be applied to one's membership fee of $500. Every effort will be made to create a schedule which accommodates a Candidate's financial needs. A Candidate must have a Sufi Order Mentor. A candidate may choose from the following: Option #1 A group retreat with a senior Sufi teacher and a 3 day personal retreat. Option #2 An individual personal program developed with one's Sufi Order guide and approved by Residence Option #3 Regular participation in a Sufi Class (Message class, Healing Class, Zikr) or a study group formed with and led by a Sufi Order Guide plus mini retreats led by one's own guide. Combinations of these options may be worked out with the Residence Committee. All requirements are to be completed within 1 year after the Candidate's acceptance.

B. Returning to Family Member Status: If a Family Member decides to leave the Abode without the intent of returning, they will become an "ex". When moving from the Abode it is important to meet with the Residence Committee to determine status as leaving in good standing or not. Also the Residence Committee is interested in feedback about experiences at the Abode. (See also Addendum #2, "Circumstances Under Which You May Be Asked To Leave the Abode," pg. 24.)

1. Non-organic herbicides, pesticides and fertilizers are not to be used on Abode land and air.

2. The Executive Committee is also to be notified prior to:

a. The use of heavy machinery (such as bulldozers, well drilling and excavation equipment) on the Land,

b. Building on or clearing land (including small buildings such as outhouses and Retreat huts),

c. Any projects that are likely to affect the eco-systems of the Abode.

6. TRASH/GARBAGE. PUT IN DUMPSTER.

THANKS VERY MUCH FOR YOUR COOPERATION!!!

C. Parking and Vehicle Regulations:

1. Short Term Parking: is described as 15 to 30 minutes, or for the purpose of loading or unloading. Parking is permitted in the driveway between Rezak and Vakil and in the driveway next to Alim. These driveways are fire lanes, so keys must be left in cars and care taken not to block others. The space in front of the barn, the spaces at the foot of the Rezak drive and below Vakil are also for short term parking. There is a $10 fine for exceeding this limit.

2. Alim Parking Lot: is reserved for the owners of apartments in Alim. Because this is a fire lane, parking for unloading or short visits is okay if the keys are left in the car.

3. Overnight Parking: may be done only in designated Abode lots, not in any driveway, not next to the main buildings, not on any road -- including along the upper parking lot and in front of the barn.

4. Unregistered Vehicles Policy: If absolutely necessary unregistered vehicles may be parked for two months free, after that there is a $10/month storage fee with prior approval of the Planning & Finance Committee.

5. Use of Abode Vehicles: Abode vehicles are to be used only by Abode employees who are named on our insurance for specific work projects. Any exceptions must be okayed by the Abode Co-ordinator, head of maintenance or person in charge of the farm.

These rules are the result of years of family discussion and agreements, as well as fire regulations. Please be open to reminding each other, in a gentle way, to keep the agreements.

D. Pond Policy: The pond is only to be used by members of the Abode and visitors, the Botez family (who live right across the road from the pond) and people living at West Family at the end of the road. This is for safety and insurance reasons as well as for Residents' comfort. Feel free to politely tell other people to leave the pond area. No child under 14 years may be at the pond without adult supervision.

NO CHILDREN ARE ALLOWED AT THE POND WITHOUT ADULT SUPERVISION.

D. Fishing: We uphold that Life is sacred and taking it should be done with respect and acknowledgement of the Spirit. Fishing may be done only by people living at the Abode and our neighbors. Children may fish when accompanied by an adult.

E. Hunting: There is absolutely NO hunting on Abode land.

10. RELATIONSHIPS

The nature of human relationship is subtle and complex, and in community this especially needs to be given the utmost attention and care. Consideration for others is felt to be the basis of any spiritual growth. Remember also that the Main Abode Complex houses Mountain Road Children's School, so our conduct should therefore be appropriate to a school atmosphere. (See Addendum #2 "Circumstances Under Which You May Be Asked To Leave the Abode," pg. 24.)

Residency: Upon joining the Abode, one becomes part of the Abode community. The friendships and connections formed among people are special and beautiful and come not only from living and working together, but also from spiritual co-evolution with each individual. It takes sensitivity and dedication to make daily interactions flow in such a large community -- tolerance, forgiveness, communication, help in times of trouble, as well as celebration in times of joy are all important. The spiritual teachings of Hazrat Inayat Khan and Pir Vilayat offer valuable guidance in the areas of relationship. There are countless opportunities to put into practice teachings such as: looking for the purpose behind the action of a friend rather than judging from a limited perspective, using honesty and delicacy in communication, mastering the impulse to add an opinion to each situation and looking for the qualities of the soul beyond the manifestation of the personality.

The Abode can be lonely and overwhelming before one gets to know others well. Try to remember how important it might be to newcomers to feel welcome.

11. UNDERSTANDINGS ABOUT INTERACTIONS WITH CHILDREN

Policies Regarding Children

Children under six (6) years of age must be within eye-sight of a responsible adult at all times.

Children fourteen (14) years of age and under must have a responsible adult in their vicinity who knows their whereabouts and is responsible for them. The child should know exactly where their "guardian" is to be found at all times.

Children's behavior towards other children

The Abode should be a safe place - physically and emotionally - for all of our children. Therefore it is expected that all children treat each other with kindness and respect.

Children's behavior toward adults

It is expected that children will respond courteously in their interaction with adults. Children need to be taught courtesy and good manners by their parents.

Adults' behavior towards children

It is expected that an adult, in speaking to a child regarding their behavior or for any other reason, will speak courteously and respectfully.

Procedures to address concerns regarding children

1. Concerns should first be addressed to the child and/or parents(s).

2. If a child does not respond appropriately, and if the behavior continues, or if the issue is not resolved to the satisfaction of all parties, community members may request mediation. Mediation is available upon request to the Residence Committee. 3. If mediation fails to resolve an issue to the satisfaction of all parties, the matter will go before the Residence Committee.

Expected behavior in the dining room

Parents have the responsibility to teach proper and appropriate behavior in the Dining Room, including the following:

Prior to meals being served, children should not hover over the food set out, nor touch the food.

During the grace circle, children should participate or sit quietly at a table.

In the serving line, only young children accompanied by a parent may go to the head of the line. All other children are expected to wait in line without pushing or cutting.

Parents need to be near enough to their children (of any age) in line to ensure the child is taking appropriate amounts of food.

Children need to sit at a table with their parents or with another adult who has agreed to eat with them.

Parents need to instruct their children in the proper clean-up of food, plates, etc., and to be sure that this is done.

Children who finish their meal before parents or who are not eating should not run and play in the dining room.

Indoor spaces in the main buildings

1. Children are welcome to be in the following indoor places as long as they are supervised by an adult in the same room. a. Family Room in Fatah b. Library in Rezak c. Cafe Qalbi

2. Children are welcome to be in the following indoor areas with a responsible adult in the same building.

a. Dining Room (this pertains to times outside of meal times. If the children are disturbing the cooks, they may be asked to leave.)

b. Mughni living room

c. Their own living spaces

3. Children may not play in the following areas under any circumstances. (However, they may be in these areas when accompanied by a responsible adult. They may also be sent on a specific errand by a responsible adult, e.g., getting a piece of fruit from the cooler).

a. The Maintenance Barn b. The Shaker Barn c. The Alim Barn d. Mountain Road School (outside of school hours. It is understood that during school hours there are responsible adults present). e. Any cellars f. The Meditation Hall g. Vakil h. The green house or any other farm buildings.

Expected Behavior Indoors (including hallways, stairways)

It is expected that parents will teach children appropriate behavior for indoor spaces. This includes no running, yelling or jumping on furniture. Appropriate activities for the Library and Dining Room are quiet activities such as reading, playing a card or board game, drawing, etc. Children need to be taught to be considerate of others who may be present.

Outdoor Spaces

There are abundant outdoor spaces for children at the Abode. Parents need to assume responsibility to be sure that their children practice safe and appropriate behavior in these spaces.

Children 14 and under must be accompanied by an adult at the pond.

Children should not play around the Meditation Hall, the Retreat Huts or the Mountain Campsite.

Children should not play in any of the flower beds.

Children may be in the Herb Garden, but it is not an appropriate play space for running, yelling, etc.

It is understood that with children under 4 years of age, and especially babies, parents will use this policy to guide their children's behavior as they grow and learn.

Treasurer's Report, February 1995

What you get for your Abode rent:

Often people look at the space where one sleeps and stores personal belongings as the exchange for rent. Rent may seem high in comparison to similar spaces outside the community, but Abode rent is part of a larger package including:

Meal preparation and clean-up as well as access to food when meals are not prepared
Maintenance and housekeeping of common buildings
Free use of Abode land and public spaces including: Meditation Hall, library, sauna, 400 acres of woodland and a pond
Utilities in Abode-owned spaces including: heat, electricity, water, sewage, hot water, trash pick-up, and now plowing.
Personal houses include: real estate taxes, snow plowing and an allotment of fire wood

Beyond all these tangible items, the Abode offers individuals an opportunity to come together in a social and spiritual community for enrichment.

Tax Issues:

It is always a good idea to consult with a tax professional when considering tax issues and how they impact your tax returns. Donations: Rent payers who are Resident consider $50/month of their rent a donation for tax purposes. It is considered a donation because it goes to support aspects of the community which are non-profit, tax-exempt functions of our spiritual or religious community. The $50 is a conservative guess in regard to the portion of Abode income which is clearly in support of the maintenance of the property for educational and religious functions. It is not an unusual amount for average Americans to contribute to support of their church. The donation does not have to be voluntary to qualify as a donation, one voluntarily elects to live here and go through the admission process. The $500 admission fee (which is also required) is also a donation.

Taxes Included in Rent: The Abode pays taxes to the town for the property which is not in the main complex of Shaker buildings (which are used for non-profit functions), and the housing in the Alim building. Specifically we pay taxes on each home based on the value we establish as the resale buyout price. These taxes may be claimed on your income tax. Check with your tax preparer.

Center Dues, Hope Project and independent donations are all tax deductible.

A Summary Statement may be requested from the Treasurer if you can use these deductions. For most residents it will be easier, and to your financial advantage, to simply take the standard deduction and ignore these items.
