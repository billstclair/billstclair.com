The Network Cache Efficiency

Let's take a look now at a large-scale piece of a possible network. Suppose I have a bunch of WIZes in my house. And one of the house WIZes has a connection to a "neighborhood" WIZ down the street. About 50 neighborhood WIZes connect to an area WIZ,  about 50 area WIZs connect to a city WIZ. All the city WIZes connect to a county WIZ, all the county WIZes connect to a state WIZ, all the state WIZes connect to a continental WIZ, which connects to a Planetary WIZ, which connects to a Solar System Wiz, etc. Lets say that this pattern is structured so that no more than say 50 WIZes ever connect to a higher level WIZ, or no WIZ supports more than 50 "children" in this hypothetical tree.

Now keep in mind these would not be the only connections. WIZes may have many connections each. Thus, my home WIZ is connected to the neighborhood WIZ, but it is also connected to my company's WIZ downtown. My family, spread out across the planet, may all connect to a central Family WIZ somewhere. Thus my home WIZ will be involved in multiple networks, each a different topology, all simultaneously overlaid on each other.

Now let's look at what is currently a worst case scenario on today's Internet: someone famous says something important and every single person on the net wants to read it within 24 hours. Suppose that's 100 million people. On the Internet, the host computer which has the data will receive 100 million "hits" and deliver it 100 million times!

But on the WIZ net, the host computer which has the data will deliver it at most 50 times, to the 50 or so other WIZs it is connected to. Each of them will in turn deliver it up to 50 times, to the 50 or so other WIZs they are each connected to, etc, etc. NO WIZ WILL DELIVER IT MORE TIMES THAN THE NUMBER OF CONNECTIONS IT HAS TO OTHER WIZES. Instead of millions of transmissions, we are talking about dozens!!!

I'll say that again! NO WIZ DELIVERS A MEMORY MORE TIMES THAN THE NUMBER OF CONNECTIONS IT HAS TO OTHER WIZES. Each of them will then cache the data and retransmit it, etc., etc.

The current Internet connection graph is structured much like a big tree. Many people connect to various local ISPs which have "trunk" lines connecting them to bigger servers, and even bigger servers, and humongous nodes that handle traffic across the ocean, etc. And at each point up the tree, the traffic multiplies to greater and greater bandwidth requirements. Thus, as we go up the hierarchy, ON THE INTERNET, THE BANDWIDTH REQUIRED INCREASES EXPONENTIALLY.

But on the WIZ central memory network, each node transmits a memory just once to each of its children, and as more and more "hits" continue occur to that page, no additional traffic occurs. In the above scenerio, where one page is published and 100 million computers read it, the bandwith used does not increase whatsever. In this case, ON THE WIZ CENTRAL MEMORY NET, THE BANDWIDTH REQUIREMENT REMAINS CONSTANT AT EVERY LEVEL!

Now while the above scenario represents an ideal and extreme case, the limiting factor still remains: NO WIZ DELIVERS A MEMORY MORE TIMES THAN THE NUMBER OF CONNECTIONS IT HAS TO OTHER WIZES!

The worst case scenario on the WIZ network is where one person sends an email to another person. It will be cached unnecessarily at every WIZ node it passes through. Fortunately, in all nodes but the final receiver, the copies will soon disappear from the caches (as unused items are deleted).

Only when you have such one-to-one data, as a person-to-person email, or when you check your account or buy a product, is their no value in caching all along the path. If all WIZ traffic were one-to-one, caching would not help any. However, looking at today's Internet let's ask this question: what percentage of the data is heading for a mass distribution? Specifically, what is the average number of hits all web pages get before they change? Whenever you access a web page which gets a million hits, you are looking at a system which has delivered that data a million times, which is about 999,950 times too many.

Finally, note that there IS a bit in each WIZ memory to tell each node it goes through not to bother caching it. When on, the efficiency is the same as the current Internet.

THUS, THE WORST CASE SCENARIO ON THE WIZ NETWORK IS EQUAL TO THE BEST CASE SCENARIO ON THE CURRENT INTERNET, AND THE BEST CASE SCENARIO ON THE WIZ NETWORK IS LITERALLY MILLIONS OF TIMES MORE EFFICIENT THAN THE CURRENT INTERNET.

And the average value is probably in the millions too, as the ratio of created data to the number of people or processes that read that data will probably always remain very high. Millions to one may be an understatement.
