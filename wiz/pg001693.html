The WIZ processor is an ISC - an "Incredibly Simple Computer"

Currently computers fall mainly into two categories:

   • CISC or "Complex Instruction Set Computer"
   • RISC or "Reduced Instruction Set Computer".

The CISC is complex, the RISC is simpler. The WIZ takes the idea of "simpler is better" to its logical conclusion and is an ISC or "Incredibly Simple Computer". Yes, I made up that name - ISC!

In the beginning the trend was for computer processors to become more and more complex. Hence CISC. The RISC reversed that trend by introducing the idea that simpler is better. Complex results can still happen on a simple machine by combining several simple operations in sequence to create one larger operation. And because a simpler machine can be made to run many times faster, the resulting several simple operations on the RISC can be quicker than the single complex operation on the CISC.

An example of this tradeoff would be something like this: suppose you have a complex machine that can add 60 digit numbers, and a simple machine that can only add 10 digit numbers. Well, the simple machine can add 60 digit numbers too -- by breaking them up into 6 groups of 10 digits each and then adding each group. It has to do 6 additions to keep up with the one addition of the complex machine. But now suppose also that this simple machine can run more than 6 times faster than the complex machine. So it can do its 6 additions actually faster than the complex machine can do its single addition. It's the proverbial six of one, half a dozen of the other.

The Intel Pentium™ is an example of a CISC gone out of control. It has become so complex that no one can handle it. Intel management can no longer coordinate its development. Software for designing it cannot keep up with needs. Intel has said it can no longer fully test the thing, and admits that more bugs and chip recalls are likely to occur. The development of newer and faster versions takes literally thousands of man-years and hundreds of millions of dollars. And their only solution to ever more demanding market pressures is to continue to increase the complexity! What fools these mortals be!

The RISC on the other hand, is easy to design, maintain, program, and test, and can very easily get faster as faster technologies are invented.

The RISC idea has come of age and proven itself. So why have companies not taken this evolution to its logical conclusion? While the RISC is not as complex as the CISC, its complexity is only "Reduced". Why only reduced? If simpler is better, let's go the full nine yards. Hence the WIZ.

The Pentium™ has hundreds of instructions. The ARM™, a very popular RISC currently, has a few dozen instructions. The WIZ has ONLY ONE instruction! It is as simple as a processor can get!

And everything good that can be said about the RISC vs. the CISC can be said even more so about the ISC (the WIZ). It is terribly easy to implement, has a very low gate count, is incredibly cheap to produce, and will run incredibly fast. And as new technologies appear (molecular computers?), implementing a WIZ on them will be very easy.

The WIZ is the exact "basic-basic" of what a computer is. It is the essence of a computer without adding anything extra that is not necessary.

It is my complete faith in the principle that "simpler is better" that leads me down the WIZ path. At every turning point, the decision has been in favor of simpler. The increased speed and manufacturability, decreased cost, and ease of moving onto future technologies will make the WIZ the processor to end all processors.

Then we can go onto other things.

So let's get on with it!
