The WIZ mind.

Computer Science has long been seeking to emulate the human mind. Their problem is, they don't actually know what a human mind is! In particular, they confuse the mind with the spirit.

In 1950, L. Ron Hubbard published the landmark book Dianetics, the Modern Science of Mental Health. Here, Hubbard defined the mind clearly for the first time. In this book, and later books, Mr. Hubbard writes:

"The mind is a communication and control system between the [being] and his environment."
"The [being] receives, by the communication system called the mind, various impressions, including direct views of the physical universe." (From The Background, Ministry, Ceremonies & Sermons of the Scientology Religion, page 206-7)

"The mind perceives and stores all data of the environment... The process of thought is the perception of the present and the comparison of it to the perceptions and conclusions of the past in order to direct action in the immediate or distant future."  (From the Axioms of Dianetics, number 64 and 65).

Mr. Hubbard showed that man is composed of three distinct parts, a body, a mind, and a spirit. It is possible for a computer to be a mind. It is possible for a computer to have a body (control a physical object). It is not possible for a computer to be a spirit, which can create, consider, and postulate. The mind cannot create, it can only compute with what is already created. Creation, consideration, and postulation are best left to living beings. By failing to realize this limitation, Computer Science has failed - it tries to emulate too much.

A WIZ can receive direction from a human (a spirit), observe its environment, analyze that environment, compare it to past environments, and figure out how to get there from here.

This is not necessarily a hard problem. For instance, the mind in a light bulb only needs to figure out whether to be on or to be off.

Again quoting Mr. Hubbard:

"In the matter of such a thing as an automatic switch, we might consider that the switch is capable of making a decision whether to be off or on. However, we must remember that the original decision that a switch was to be made, and that "off" and "on" could be accomplished and, indeed, the design of the switch itself depended entirely upon life quality."

"In the field of mechanics we do not discover creativeness."

"Where mechanics have ability, the ability is only apparent and has been endowed into the mechanics by life. It is all right to suppose that an electronic brain (computer) is capable of thought as long as one realizes that life itself must necessarily be present in order to give cause and quality, or direction, to such a brain. An electronic brain will sit all day and do nothing unless life starts the machine running. It will give millions of answers, but none of these, no matter how sharp, have any meaning until they are viewed by life. The machine is never anything more than a servomechanism to life. Indeed, a machine cannot even exist in the absence of life. (from The Background, Ministry, Ceremonies & Sermons of the Scientology Religion, p194-5)

Additionally, in Dianetics,  Mr. Hubbard published a great deal of data on the algorithm by which the mind works. It is my intention to use this algorithm as given in this book as the basis of a software program which will compute much as the human mind does.

Thus, all the day to day objects we interact with, anything that is mechanical, electrical, anything that moves, any tool, any thing, will become an intelligent  thing -- a servomechanism to life.
