The Four Modes of Comms

Generally speaking, a comm operates in one of four modes; although these modes are not rigidly defined -- there are other modes, and a comm can transition seamlessly from any mode to any other.

1. Completely independent mode

A comm can mock up any synthetic image and speak in any synthesized voice, and pretend to have any "personality" it wants to have. It's just a computer program.

2. Imitating a human mode

A comm can mock up a synthetic image that looks just like some known real person, and speak in a synthesized voice that imitates that same real person's voice, and even try to act with the valence (personality) of that same person. Generally, this would occur with the permission of and actually at the behest of that person. (I don't want to get into the legal issues here, only the technical possibilities. Obviously, using encrypted digital signatures, it would always be possible to distinguish a comm from a human. Also, a comm can't shake your hand!).

3. Live projection mode

A comm can easily mock up being an exact duplicate of a human at a remote location. That is, a human at place A is recorded live, and this live recording is transmitted to remote location B in real-time, and played back (projected) at location B. I am here, but you see me projected there. This is essentially a "video-phone". From my point of view, I am projecting myself to a remote spot. A comm in this mode is not an independent operator but merely a projection of the original speaker.

4. Recorded projection mode

A comm can replay a previously recorded live projection. There is no observable difference between a comm duplicating a human live right now (live projection mode), and a comm re-playing that same duplication at a later time when the original human is no longer there. Again, a comm in this mode is not an independent operator but merely a time-delayed projection of the original speaker.
