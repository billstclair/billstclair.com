The accounting on transmissions is actually very simple. Remember that it is a recursive, cellular network. A WIZ only deals with those WIZes it is connected to, a number on the order of a few dozen in my examples. Thus, if A asks B for data, and B asks C, and C asks D, and D asks E, then E charges D for the transmission, D charges C, C charges B, and B charges A. Each knows about only one hop of the transmission. Each does not know about any final destination (ie, A) other than the direct peer it is transmitting to. Each may pass along their cost to the next. Thus, E charges D a penny, D charges C two pennies, C charges B three pennies, and B charges A four pennies. All A is aware of is that it asked B for data and got it from B at a cost of four pennies.

Remember, the network IS the infrastructure. That is, in the above jump from A to E, B, C, and D are not necessilary part of the infrastructure, they are just other nodes in a large self-configuring peer-to-peer network, to use now terminology.

In the above example, A would only have accounts with B and a few dozen other B-primes not mentioned, ie, the WIZes it was connected to. It never deals with C, D, or E, or even knows of their existence. Thus, A deals only with a few dozen accounts.

And note that a WIZ can keep adding up the fees all day long and not actually "submit" a banking transaction until some dollar or time limit is reached.

Also, note that the WIZ memory scheme makes the accounting system trivial. If B is serial number 123456 and has a memory named WIZ-account-123456-balance, then it is simple to add or subtract a penny from that memory. The program is not much more complex than the single statement:

     let <<WIZ-account-123456-balance>> = <<WIZ-account-123456-balance>> + 0.01

Actually, to give more detail to this, the memory "WIZ-account-123456-balance" is actually owned by a third entity, ie, a "bank". It issues "vouchers" in the form of digitally encrypted keys to the account. Thus, the real call is something more like

    call bank ( <<my-account-voucher>> , <<his-account-voucher>> , 0.01)

which subtracts 0.01 from my-account and pays it to his-account, given all the vouchers decrypt properly. And keep in mind that "bank" is a memory too, and can execute on any WIZ. The executing WIZ finds the subroutine (memory) "bank" in its own memory, the central memory. In the WIZ memory net, any memory can execute on any processor, there is no "home" or "domain" for a memory.

Thus, the software is not "complex (and expensive) .. to build and keep an accounting system especially for small amount transactions."

Another note: if WIZ A is connected to B, and both are owned by the same financial entity, they don't have to charge each other. There is no problem with a free transmission. Thus, within any corporate structure, all WIZ transmissions would be free as you suggest.

And any WIZ can set its own rates, therefore any WIZ CAN set a rate of zero. The memory handler algorithm is constantly searching to optimize the route it takes, and depending on the parameter of whether you want to optimize speed or optimize cost, can take different routes. Thus, a free infrastructure could be set up and coexist with a private infrastructure, you take your choice. The free nodes may be slow and few and far between, whereas private WIZes with big disks and fast comm lines may spring up wherever people are willing to pay more.

It may become true that at some point in history communications will become so cheap as to be subsidizable out of some sort of overhead fund (taxes?). That is the same as saying that the free WIZes may handle traffic so well there would be no market for private nodes.

However, we are not there yes, and I am a big believer in making every part of this system equitable and self-sustaining in a democratic capitalist sort of way. And since the mechanism just described is relatively simple, I would implement it. If prices should go down to zero, then the system will phase itself out.

Regarding the battery running out. WIZes run forever! Seriously! I will have a chapter on this soon. There are two pieces to this: low power requirements in the first place, and new kinds of power sources in the second. A WIZ processor can slow down to zero Hz, and wake on interrupt. Power usage is exactly linear to speed. At absolute zero Hz, it will use absolutely no power. It can sweep its cache RAM out to the Central Memory, and then doesn't even have to keep a D-RAM powered. Many WIZ applications can sleep most of the time. If I had a WIZ in my shoe. for instance, and it had a MEMS accellerometer to sense footsteps, it could wake just for a few nanoseconds at the peak of each footstep to record the data, then sleep until the next footstep. (Actually, it would be more interesting than that - it could change its speed in something like an adaptive sine wave, relating speed on a moment-by-moment basis to what its doing). A WIZ-light switch would only execute when the switch handle was jarred. (It might run at 100 Hz while waiting, just so it could keep monitoring everything). All WIZs slow down the CPU while waiting for an external event to wake it. In fact, whenever the processor goes into a "wait state" for I/O, it can cycle down speed and wake on interrupt from the device in question. Thus, power used will be often miniscule. (Its in a different chapter - "The WIZ-object", but most WIZes will be inside shoes, switches, staplers, whatever. Not "windows" machines running screens with multiple processes all going like crazy). Obviously, on a cpu bound application, this is not true, but that kind of application will be plugged in!

Secondly, I will be investigating a number of new kinds of power supplies. Since all isolated WIZes will have a radio link, they can pick up power over that link by using the radio signal itself as power (as Tesla always planned, also ala these toy "perputual motion" machines which have a little coil and absorb power inductively from the 60 Hz AC fields that exist all around us). It may be possible to keep powered by this alone. It may be possible to "recharge" it occasionally with a higher than normal burst of radio power (at night or when no humans are around!).

Beyond that, new power sources have yet to be discoverred. I believe cold fusion is a real phenomonon, and will be perfected. It could be applied on a MEMS nano-scale to make a cold fusion reactor a few nanometers big. I have also read the very theoretical idea of creating a "bowling alley" sort of MEMS structure a few nanometers wide, and accellerating single particles down the alley to a target, thus creating a very tiny but super-high energy particle accellerator, which would create hot (thermo-nuclear) fusion, but in non-dangerous quantities of a single atom at a time. And room-temperature superconducting nanotubes are right around the corner already.

The WIZ is a device for the future, and I am certain that the future holds plenty of possibilities for self-powered machines.... But that is another chapter.
