The simplest common WIZ-Objects

Keep in mind that a WIZ is very tiny, say the size of a grain of rice, and very cheap to produce, say a dime. It is self powered and radio connected. Thus, no wiring is required to place one remotely with any object.

Some WIZ-objects may contains many parts, and each of those parts may be themselves WIZ-objects. As all coordination between the various WIZ-object parts is done in software, all wiring and mechanical linkage between the various parts can be eliminated.

Here I will present some of the simpler WIZ-objects, out of which larger and more complex objects may be constructed.

The WIZ-Motor

A DC motor run by a WIZ can run more reliably, use less power, and develop more torque, than any other kind of motor. By using a series of electromagnets powered sequentially by the WIZ, a degree of control far more sophisticated than current DC motor "controller chips" can be attained.

The basic product here is a shaft that spins. Again, there would be many models of different size, power requirements, etc. Any model should be able to stop, start, attain any speed from zero to its maximum, forward or backward, etc, under WIZ control.

Additionally there are WIZ-stepper motors. A stepper motor is not much different, except that instead of spinning round and round, it spins only a maximum of a single turn, and can stop and hold its position at any specified angle.

The WIZ-fluid flow valve

The WIZ-valve looks like a piece of tubing which comes in various sizes from say 1/16" to 2" in diameter, and from 1 to 4 inches in length. In the center is a bulge or bubble, which houses a WIZ and the mechanism to open and close a valve inside, which goes from 0% (closed) to 100% (fully open). Both ends of the tube would have standard connectors or threads, for attaching these to longer sections of ordinary tubing, hoses, or pipes. There may also be various models for use with different fluids, temperature and pressure ranges, and required speed of operation. Hopefully a rugged technology can be developed to handle a wide range of options with a single or a few models, but some specialty valves will probably always be needed for extreme operations. There should be a great deal of compatibility and interchangeability between the various models, internal parts should be the same, and mass-manufacturing should make them terribly cheap.

The WIZ-fluid flow sensor

Looks just like a WIZ-fluid flow valve, but instead of a valve, it has a sensor which senses the flow rate, temperature, pressure, etc (eg, viscosity) of the fluid flowing through it. These may be combined with WIZ-fluid flow valves in a single unit also (although separating them gives a redundant check of the valve's operation).

The WIZ-solenoid (lever, actuator)

Another common part is a solenoid, a lever which extends in and out under electromagnetic control. These would control lots of simple movement, levers, door latches, etc. Again, many sizes and shapes, but kept minimal so that mass production can occur. As more objects convert to using WIZ-objects, more standardization will occur, so a few shapes and sizes should be recommended so that new designs will gravitate toward a smaller set of various parts.

It may be easier and/or more efficient to use a WIZ-stepper motor with a worm gear built inside rather than a solenoid. Whichever...

The WIZ- Accelerometer (motion detector, switch)

For the most part, switches in a WIZ world will be unnecessary. However, since a MEMS accelerometer is very simple and cheap to product, a WIZ-accelerometer should be very useful. It can be used to replace micro-switches, as any slight touch will trigger the device. It can be used to detect the end-of-travel, various positions, etc. And it can be used as a standard switch, detecting when it is pushed.

The WIZ-eye

A WIZ with a built-in CCD array should be very cheap and terribly useful in a very wide variety of applications. For instance, instead of using an accelerometer as a micro-switch to detect things like end-of-travel position of something, we could just "look" at it with the WIZ-eye.
