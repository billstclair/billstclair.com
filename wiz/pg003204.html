The WIZ Processor - In Summary

In summary, I have defined a processor that is terribly simple. Incredibly simple. The ISC. Its only operation is to copy one register to another. It has only 7 basic registers, as follows:

 
RAMDATA /INCADR
RAMADR /READ, /ADDER
RAMXADR /STACK
STACK  
FBREG /0, /1, /SAME
CONST /0B, /1B, /2B, /3B, /4B
SKIPIF /NONZERO, /ZERO
The WIZ cycle is terribly fast. Register to register transfers run at a very great speed with few levels of logic between them, and those things that are by nature not so fast are partitioned out of the main loop and into function blocks, whose architecture allows parallelism to be exploited for maximum speed there.

The basic questions to ask are:

  1. Could anything be removed? That is, have I reached "basic-basic", the fundamental minimum set of operations that still creates a useful machine?
  2. Is there any operation a Pentium can do that a straightforward set of WIZ instructions can't do?
  3. Do all decompositions of complex operators into a sequence of smaller ones result in going just as fast or faster? (At least for those complex operators found in a device like the Pentium).
This last question is answered YES, as follows:

THE FUNDAMENTAL (GUIDING) HYPOTHESIS OF THE ISC WIZ:

Any complex logical operation can be reduced to a series of simpler operations, some in series, some in parallel, whose total timing is essentially the same as the timing of the more complex operation they replace.
I suspect that the proof of this would be similar to the proof that any logical equation can be expressed in canonical sum of products form, yes?

COROLLARY TO THE FUNDAMENTAL HYPOTHESIS:

The reduced complexity and reduced chip area gained by decomposing complex operations into simpler ones creates higher overall speed of the simpler operations and therefore a net gain in speed even over the complex operations is achieved.
I rest my case! Simplicity works! Simpler is faster. Simpler is cheaper. Simpler is easier to manufacture. Simpler is easier to migrate to new technologies. Simpler is, well, ... simpler.
