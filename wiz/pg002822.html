Using the WIZ Central Memory to coordinate several WIZ-objects

The concept of a shared Central Memory allows WIZes to communicate simply by agreeing on the name (ie, address) of a single memory. One WIZ stores something into that memory, another looks to see what is there.

For instance, suppose there was a WIZ-light switch, serial number 1738493. Suppose it agrees to store either the word "ON" or the word "OFF" in a WIZ memory called "light switch number 1738493 status".

Then when the switch is moved, all the software running in that WIZ-light switch has to do is to store either "ON" or "OFF" into "light switch number 1738493 status". This programming is terribly simple. The entire purpose of this WIZ is to store either ON or OFF into that memory.

Now, any other WIZ anywhere in the universe can look at "light switch number 1738493 status" and see what is there.

For instance, suppose there was a WIZ-light bulb somewhere. It is not connected to any switch. It goes on and off when it pleases, so to speak. It is told to monitor light switch #1738493 and go on or off whenever that switch indicates to. All the software running in that WIZ-light bulb has to do is look at a memory called "light switch number 1738493 status".

Note that the switch and the light bulb could be in the same room, different rooms, or on opposite ends of the galaxy. It wouldn't matter, as the WIZ system handles the Central Memory, and neither WIZ is "aware" of the mechanics of how the transfer of information actually takes place.

This means that there are NO WIRES interconnecting things. A non-WIZ switch has to have a wire between it and the light bulb it controls. A WIZ-object has no wires other than power (if necessary) or a Central Memory communications line (if necessary). Many will use a radio link for their comm line to the Central Memory, and many also can use the power line as a comm line also. And many will be self-powered and radio-linked, completely eliminating any wiring.

There is an immense reduction in complexity by removing object-to-object wiring, along with a tremendous increase in flexibility. For instance, look at a car door with electric door locks. Each door has a switch to lock and unlock the door, which is wired to the solenoid that actually moves the locking mechanism up and down. But typically, the master switch at the driver's door controls all other doors, thus requiring a wire to go from that switch to each of the other three doors. Add to this the wires going back and forth for electric windows, speakers, and much more, and you get a mess of hundreds of wires that no one can understand. If all devices were WIZ devices, there would be only a power buss, and all other coordination between devices would be controlled by software.
