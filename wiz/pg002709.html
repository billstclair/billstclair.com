I want a patent on the word "HEY!"

Speaking of audio communication, here's something I want to do with today's telephone (year 2000). I want a "look-and-feel" patent on the command used to tell the telephone to "call" another, and the sequence of actions that then takes place. Current phones just coming out with voice command use the word "DIAL" or "CALL" as their command. Thus, the current process goes something like this:

 
I say: "PHONE -- CALL JOE!".
Joe's phone goes: "BEEP ... BEEP ... BEEP"
Joe says: "JOE HERE! WHO'S THERE?"
I say: "HI JOE, IT'S STEVE."
Joe says: "HI STEVE! WHAT'S UP?"

Finally, the real conversation begins:
 
"I say: "WHAT TIME ARE WE GETTING TOGETHER TONIGHT?"
Joe says: "HOW ABOUT 7 PM?"
I say: "GREAT, SEE YOU THEN."

Now, suppose we change the command from "PHONE -- DIAL" to "HEY". Thus, "HEY JOE" not "DIAL JOE" is how you call him. And then, suppose the phone actually records your voice as you say "HEY JOE", and continues to record anything else you might say after that, until the time Joe "picks up". Now when Joe does pick up his phone, we don't go live immediately. Instead, his phone plays to him the recording of me just made, saying "HEY JOE" and whatever else I said after that. And then we go live.

Now, my end of the conversation goes like this:

 
I say: "HEY JOE! STEVE HERE. WHAT TIME ARE WE GETTING TOGETHER TONIGHT?"
After a short pause,
I hear Joe say:		 "HI STEVE. HOW ABOUT 7 PM?"
I say: "GREAT, SEE YOU THEN."
And what Joe actually hears goes like this:
 
Joe's phone rings: "BEEP ... BEEP ... BEEP"
Joe hears me say
(via recording):		 "HEY JOE! STEVE HERE. WHAT TIME ARE WE GETTING TOGETHER TONIGHT?"
Joe immediately says: "HI STEVE. HOW ABOUT 7 PM?"
I say (live): "GREAT, SEE YOU THEN."
Note that when I actually say "Hey Joe! Steve Here. What time are we getting together tonight?", Joe has not yet answered. I am just talking to a phone. Then when Joe picks up, he will then hear it via time delayed recording.

ANOTHER FEATURE: RECORDINGS REPLACE ANSWERING MACHINES:

Now, suppose after saying "HEY JOE! DON'T FORGET TO MEET ME TONIGHT AT 7PM!", Joe doesn't answer, and after a minute I hang up. Whenever Joe does pick up his phone, a minute later, an hour later, a month later, he still hears me say it.

And after hearing it, anything Joe might speak into the phone becomes a reply. That is, Joe hears me say "HEY JOE! DON'T FORGET TO MEET ME TONIGHT AT 7PM!". Joe doesn't care at this point if I am still hanging on the line waiting for a reply, or if I said this and hung up hours ago. Joe merely replies, "OK! BUT I'LL BE A BIT LATE, LET'S MAKE IT ABOUT 7:30, OK?" If I am still on the line, we'll then be live and can continue. If I am not still on the line, the phone calls me back again! When and if I answer, I hear the delayed recording of "OK! BUT I'LL BE A BIT LATE, LET'S MAKE IT ABOUT 7:30, OK?", and then we go live again. If he has hung up in the meantime, it will still record whatever I reply and eventually he will get it and reply back, and so on.

Thus, Joe and I can talk back and forth, with no real difference between whether we reply within a second to each other, or within a day, month, or year! We are simply talking back and forth.

This could be implemented today on today's phones. It's a look-and-feel issue -- when you say "HEY JOE!" instead of "DIAL JOE" you feel as if you are talking to Joe rather than talking to a phone. The important thing here is that the phone itself DISAPPEARS. It isn't part of the process. It could be hidden. It isn't important.

And I want the patent rights!
