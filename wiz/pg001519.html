The car: an example of how the WIZ changes the way we will design mechanical objects.

In addition to eliminating inter-object wires, the WIZ Central Memory also eliminates many types of mechanical connections. Often several things are connected to each other by levers, so that when one moves so will the other. These interconnecting levers, and also camshafts, belts, pulleys, etc, are often replaced by software concepts of self-synchronization. The automobile engine is an excellent example of this kind of mechanical linkage.

Let's look at how a car could be designed differently, still keeping the gasoline engine (which I would never want to do - but this is a demonstration).

Start with the gas pedal. It is a pedal-WIZ! It has no mechanical or electrical connections to any other part of the car. It's just a piece of plastic with a spring mount which knows how hard it is being stomped on. It is self powered and radio connected. It has no wires. Its software is very very simple. It continuously stores how hard it is being stomped on (as a number from zero to 100) into a Central Memory location named "gas pedal #938475 stomp factor".

Elswhere in the same car (I hope its in the same car - could get some funny results if it was in a different car) is a WIZ-valve on a gasoline line. Its software is very very simple too. It just continuously reads the number in "gas pedal #938475 stomp factor" and adjusts its own self to open exactly the same percentage.

The steering wheel is just a cheap plastic wheel-WIZ that can sense and communicate its rotations. No wires, no connections. Like the slapstick in a Laurel and Hardy movie, you could pull it off and rotate it in free space and it would still work. It just stores it's angle of rotation (zero to 360) in a memory named "steering wheel # 3748596 rotation angle".

On each wheel is a WIZ-motor which steers the wheel. This is a heavy duty stepper motor which can change the forward angle of each wheel independantly. All four wheels read the value of "steering wheel # 3748596 rotation angle" and determine what to do accordingly. This software is quite a bit more complex than the previous example, because the wheels must also look at other memory variables, like the "road wetness #234876 guage", and must coordinate between them so that both front wheels don't start going in different directions.

Today's steering wheel is a very heavy-duty object. It has sophisticated mounting to the car body, can raise and lower its position to suit driver comfort, and is connected to numerous electrical, mechanical, and hydraulic systems. It costs hundreds of dollars to repair or replace. A WIZ steering wheel could be a cheap plastic toy mounted on a bolt!

The radiator fan of course is a WIZ which turns itself on or off when it decides it should. It works in conjunction with a WIZ-temperature sensor placed nearby. Keep in mind that there are no wires between these various WIZ-objects. Their coordination depends on software, which is terribly simple, because each WIZ-object need only calculate its piece of the puzzle. For example, the radiator fan WIZ need only look at the temperature as stored by the nearby temperature sensor and turn on if it crosses some threshold. The software is hardly more complex then that. Thus, by seperating the hardware into seperate WIZ processors, the individual processor's algorithms become very simple, and a cellular operation takes effect, the whole (car) being greater than the sum of its parts!

Now let's look at the engine itself. Take a piston, put it into a cylinder. Build in several WIZ-valves to allow gasoline and air to enter, and exhaust products to exit. Add a WIZ-spark plug. Connect the gas and air and exhaust hoses, and the spark plug power wire. And that's it! Put one on each wheel!

What is missing from this picture? There are no camshafts overhead. There are no throttle control cables. There is no timing chain. There are no control rods. There is no distributor. These are the physical linkages between things: wires, levers, pulleys, rods, cams - that cause coordination of the movement of the various parts. Like the wiring between switch and light, they are gone!

In short, about 90% of the mass of a car engine is missing. And 99% of the complexity. A piston is a very simple device. Its just a rod inside a tube. You let some gas in, spark it, it pushes out, and that's it! But look at all the "stuff" that gets added to it to make it "work"!

In short, the total number of parts to a car will go down by 90% or more. A car becomes simple. A couple of wheels mounted on a couple of axles with a motor. It is simple, like the first Model-A Fords used to be.

And what advantage is there now to putting a bunch of pistons together in a single block anyway? Why not put one simple piston on each wheel? No more crankshaft! No more transmission!! Another few hundred pounds of dead weight gone!

The point here is to look at just how much of a complex device like a car engine is really LINKAGE and CONTROL MECHANISMS, as opposed to the simple mechanism of the engine itself.

With WIZes, mechanical objects become just their "essences", with no added "stuff". No linkage, no control mechanisms, no wires. Nice and simple. Their software remains simple because each WIZ-object only needs to coordinate those things that directly involve itself. And the WIZ Central Memory system handles all the details of communication between objects.

Simplicity = magic!
