The WIZ chip as a replacement for all electronics.

The WIZ microprocessor chip is a new kind of design based on the principle of Simplicity. It is the "basic-basic" of what a microprocessor chip is. Its design, because it is so simple, lends itself to very high speed, low cost, mass manufacturing, and ease of migration to future technologies.

Additionally, the WIZ processor design lends itself very greatly to "system-on-a-chip" integration, meaning reducing entire PC Boards, or circuits with several chips and other parts, into a single chip.

High speed and a new software methodology will cause most functions done currently by custom hardware to move to pure software. As this occurs, there will be less and less reason for custom hardware.

Thus, we have a single chip that can master all things. Instead of designing a chip for this, a chip for that, and so on, we will have just one (or a few varieties of one) chip that does all.

I'd like to examine for a moment the economics of hardware vs. software function blocks. When I speak of solving the "system-on-a-chip" integration problems, I mean by using the WIZ hardware function block architecture. This architecture allows simple connection of inter-changeable inter-operable pieces of hardware such as disk controllers, modems, special digital signal processing, etc.

The current geopolitical economics of chip making causes each and every little company with a specific hardware need to design their own custom chip, or design a small board around a configurable but pretty specific chip. For example, a toy company wants a toy that speaks, they don't use a fully programmable general purpose processor like a Pentium™ or even an ARM™ RISC, they custom design something, maybe around a TI TMS5220 speech chip. The job could be done complete with software on a general purpose processor, but a "simpler" hardware solution is chosen.

There are several "mistakes" here.

1. Hardware seems simpler than software. This is true only to people who know hardware and don't know software, or to people who work for MicroSoft. Don't tell them, but it just doesn't have to be that way!

2. A general purpose machine just can't keep up with the speed required of a custom circuit. True for a small class of circuits, and becoming less true for fewer and fewer circuits every day. With a WIZ at 10x or 100x current Pentiums, this argument will all but go away. There will always be a few special circuits that need even more speed, but this is very very limited.

3. A small custom chip with limited function is cheaper than a large general purpose device. This is true now because of inverted economics. (A) the general purpose devices are designed so badly they have driven their own price/performance ratio out of this world. The WIZ restores this by giving us a really fast high performance general purpose device at a tiny cost. And (B), because of the economies of scale vs. the cost of starting up, each individual custom chip see's no cost advantage to the more general purpose solution, but if all the individual custom chips combined into a single chip, their total production level would bring that price down quickly.

Thus, the WIZ provides an umbrella under which so many more custom chips can shed their hardware and become software only, and the mass production attained by having a single but general purpose WIZ will keep costs fabulously low. I don't see why a WIZ has to cost more than a nickel to manufacture!

In general, there is an illusion that if you need a very limited capability (compared to a Pentium™) then it is cheaper to build a custom circuit with just that capability then to use a general purpose processor.
 
 

Think of anything you can think of that currently has electronic parts in it. Think of all those parts being replaced by a single WIZ chip. The simplification is enormous. Things get smaller, simpler to handle, simpler to repair, cheaper, and much much smarter!

There will be no more radios: only speakers with WIZs in them. Anti-lock brakes are just brakes with WIZs in them. There will be no more switches, knobs, or dials. ANd no more wires between things. A microwave oven will be JUST a microwave oven, ie, just a box with a door and a microwave generator inside. It won't have a control panel and display with the time still blinking 12:00. All electronics, all controls, all displays, will disappear. Only the basic object will be left. With a WIZ inside.

These are my new race of creatures, little guys I call WIZes.

Additionally, as things get simpler but smarter, there will be less need for so many different things. In a WIZ future, there will be less technology, not more. Fewer tools, fewer devices, fewer parts to things.

Simpler, not more complex.
