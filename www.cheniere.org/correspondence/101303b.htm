For measurement, thermodynamics is very straightforward: The output work divided by the operator's input energy gives the COP as a decimal. The output work divided by the total energy input (the operator's input plus any additional input by the active environment) gives the system efficiency (usually stated as a percentage). No system has efficiency greater than 100%, else it would have to "create" energy.

On the other hand, nature certainly permits COP > 1.0, even in systems with efficiency of 50% or less, which means in systems which waste half or more of their total input energy. In the familiar heat pump, e.g., its efficiency may be 50% or so, but its COP under decent conditions will be COP = 3.0 to 4.0.  The extra energy, of course, is freely input by the external environment after the unit extracts the heat from it. A solar cell, e.g., has a COP = infinity, because the operator input is zero. An average one may have an efficiency of only 17%, however, which means it wastes 83% of all the energy it receives from the environment.
